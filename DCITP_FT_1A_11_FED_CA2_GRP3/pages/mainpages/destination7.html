About Chinatown

Chinatown is one of Singapore's most historic and culturally rich districts, centred around Pagoda, Trengganu, Sago, Temple, and Smith Streets in the Outram area. Once the heart of Chinese settlement in colonial Singapore, the district today blends restored shophouses, street markets, hawker centres, and significant religious sites, including a Buddhist temple, a Hindu temple, and a Hokkien temple standing within a short walk of one another. Chinatown remains a major draw for both tourists and locals, especially during Chinese New Year, when the streets are decorated with thousands of lanterns and festive light-ups.

History

Chinatown's origins trace back to Sir Stamford Raffles' 1822 Town Plan, which designated the area southwest of the Singapore River as a settlement for Chinese immigrants. From 1843, land leases and grants for homes and trade were awarded around Pagoda Street, Temple Street, Smith Street, Trengganu Street, and Sago Street, and rows of shophouses were built along these lanes to house new arrivals and their businesses. Over the following century, the district grew into a dense, self-contained community with clan associations, temples, and traditional trades. In the 1980s, many street hawkers were relocated into permanent structures such as the Chinatown Complex, and today Chinatown is a conservation area that preserves its historic shophouse streetscape alongside modern shopping and dining.

About

One of Singapore's most iconic hawker centres, Maxwell Food Centre sits at the junction of Maxwell Road and South Bridge Road. It is best known for Tian Tian Hainanese Chicken Rice, along with a wide range of Chinese, Malay, and Indian stalls serving affordable local dishes.

Chinatown Street Market

Overview

The Chinatown Street Market is the pedestrianised heart of the district, spanning Pagoda, Trengganu, Sago, Temple, and Smith Streets. Rows of stalls beneath red lanterns sell souvenirs, silk, tea, traditional crafts, and snacks, making it one of Singapore's most photographed shopping stretches and the natural starting point for exploring Chinatown.

Famous Landmarks Within Chinatown

Pagoda Street

Location: Chinatown, between South Bridge Road and Trengganu Street

Opening Hours: Open 24 hours (stalls typically operate 9:30 AM - 10:00 PM)

Rating: 4.4⭐

Price: Free to walk

Named after the gopuram (pagoda-like tower) of the nearby Sri Mariamman Temple, Pagoda Street was one of the earliest streets settled by Chinese immigrants in the 1840s. It was converted into a pedestrian mall in 1997 and is now lined with souvenir stalls and heritage shophouses.

Chinatown Heritage Centre

Location: 48 Pagoda Street, Singapore 059207

Opening Hours: 10:00 AM - 8:00 PM, Daily

Rating: 4.6⭐

Price: Paid admission (check official website for current rates)

Housed in a row of restored shophouses on Pagoda Street, the Chinatown Heritage Centre uses recreated living quarters, artefacts, and multimedia guides to tell the story of early Chinese immigrant life in Singapore, including cramped tenement housing and traditional trades.

Trengganu Street

Location: Chinatown, connecting Sago Street and Pagoda Street

Opening Hours: Open 24 hours (stalls typically operate 9:30 AM - 10:00 PM)

Price: Free to walk

Named after the Malaysian state of Trengganu, this street was once home to the Lai Chun Yuen opera house and later a cinema. Today it crosses Pagoda Street to form the busiest junction of the Chinatown Street Market, popular for its lantern displays and street food.

Other Features

  • Rows of restored pre-war shophouses along conserved streets
  • Seasonal light-ups and street bazaars during Chinese New Year
  • Traditional trades: tea houses, dried goods, and Chinese medicine shops
  • Direct access to Chinatown MRT Station
  • Indoor malls nearby, including People's Park Complex and Chinatown Point
Attractions

Buddha Tooth Relic Temple & Museum

Details

Location: Buddha Tooth Relic Temple & Museum

Address: 288 South Bridge Road, Singapore 058840

Opening Hours: 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM, Daily

Rating: 4.6⭐

Price: Free (Donations are Welcomed)

About

Completed in 2007 and designed in the architectural style of China's Tang dynasty, this temple and museum houses what devotees believe to be a sacred tooth relic of the Buddha in a gold stupa on its upper floor, alongside galleries of Buddhist art and a rooftop garden.

Sri Mariamman Temple

Details

Location: Sri Mariamman Temple

Address: 244 South Bridge Road, Singapore 058793

Opening Hours: 6:00 AM - 12:00 PM & 6:00 PM - 9:00 PM, Daily

Rating: 4.5⭐

Price: Free (Donations are Welcomed)

About

Singapore's oldest Hindu temple, founded in 1827 by early Tamil immigrant Naraina Pillai. Gazetted a National Monument in 1973, it is instantly recognisable by its ornately sculpted gopuram tower entrance and hosts the annual Thimithi fire-walking festival.

Thian Hock Keng Temple

Details

Location: Thian Hock Keng Temple

Address: 158 Telok Ayer Street, Singapore 068613

Opening Hours: 7:30 AM - 5:30 PM, Daily

Rating: 4.5⭐

Price: Free (Donations are Welcomed)

About

Built between 1839 and 1842, Thian Hock Keng is Singapore's oldest and most important Hokkien temple, dedicated to the sea goddess Mazu. Constructed in traditional Southern Chinese style without a single nail, it was gazetted a National Monument in 1973.
